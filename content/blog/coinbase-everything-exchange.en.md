Wall Street expected a profit on an adjusted basis. Coinbase printed a **loss of $1.49 per share** in the first quarter of **2026** against expectations for roughly **27 cents** of earnings.

Revenue was **$1.41 billion**, below consensus near **$1.52 billion**. Shares slipped about **4%** in late trading.

On paper the story wrote itself — Bitcoin slid about **22%** in the quarter — and exchanges live and die with the tape.

The reported loss is more complicated than a single operating miss. Accounting rules require Coinbase to mark large crypto holdings to market at quarter-end. That can swing GAAP results between large losses and large gains without a single coin changing hands. It is not necessarily a clean read on day-to-day business momentum.

## “Everything exchange” — strategy born from crisis

More interesting than the backward quarter is what Coinbase is building in response.

CEO Brian Armstrong previously outlined a vision he called the **“everything exchange”** — a platform less dependent on spot trading in a handful of tokens, and more focused on infrastructure for **any** digitally tradable asset class.

In Q1 **2026** that vision shows early traction in the data:

- **Derivatives** volume reached **$4.2 billion**, up **169%** year over year
- Prediction markets, launched in partnership with Kalshi in January, are on track toward roughly **$100 million** in annualized revenue by year-end **2026**
- **USDC-related** revenue rose to **$305 million** from **$274 million** in the year-ago quarter

“We’re diversifying what people can trade so there’s always something someone wants,” CFO Alesia Haas told CNBC — framing mix shift as a volatility dampener relative to pure crypto spot cycles.

## Seven hundred jobs and AI efficiency

Three days before earnings, Coinbase announced it would cut about **14%** of staff — roughly **700 roles**. Management linked the move both to softer trading volumes and to faster internal adoption of AI workflows.

The underlying problem is not “solved.” Coinbase remains deeply tethered to crypto beta, and crypto remains structurally difficult to forecast for multi-year earnings models.

Yet Q1’s metrics suggest diversification is generating **measurable** outcomes — not intentions alone — even if headline GAAP punished the tape on release.
